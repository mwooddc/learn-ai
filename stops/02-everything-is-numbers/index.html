Stop 2 · Everything is numbers

How does a computer see, hear and read?

At the end of Stop 1, a picture of a cat turned out to be 256 numbers. That wasn't a trick. A computer can't do anything with a picture, a sound or a word until it has been turned into numbers. You're going to do the turning.

Part 1 · Pictures

Paint with numbers

A digital picture is a grid of tiny squares called pixels. In a black-and-white picture, each pixel is just one number: how bright it is. 0 is black, 255 is white, and everything in between is a shade of grey.

Paint on the left grid. Watch the right grid, which shows what the computer actually stores.

Try this: paint the first letter of your name. Then try to make something look curved, using only squares and a few greys.

Why stop at 255?Go deeper

Inside a computer, every number is stored with tiny switches that are either off or on. A group of 8 switches can be set in 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 256 different ways, so it can store any number from 0 to 255. A group of 8 switches is called a byte.

256 shades of grey turns out to be enough that your eye can't see the steps between them.

Part 2 · Resolution

How many numbers?

Your 12 × 12 grid was tiny. Real pictures use far more pixels. Drag the slider to change how many pixels this picture gets.

8 × 8 = 64 numbers

What's the smallest grid where you can still tell it's a cat? Your brain is incredibly good at this. Remember that, because an AI has to work out "cat" from nothing but these numbers.

A typical phone photo is about 4,000 × 3,000 pixels. That's 12 million pixels, and it's in colour, which makes it bigger still. Keep going to see why.

Part 3 · Colour

Colour is three numbers

Look at a screen through a magnifying glass and you'll see that every pixel is really three tiny lights: red, green and blue. Each light has a brightness from 0 to 255. Mix them and you can make any colour.

So each pixel of a colour photo is three numbers, not one. That 12-million-pixel phone photo? 36 million numbers.

Why does red + green make yellow?Go deeper

Mixing paint and mixing light work differently. Paint absorbs light, so mixing paints takes colours away and gets darker. A screen gives out light, so mixing adds colours together and gets brighter.

Your eye has three kinds of colour sensor, roughly tuned to red, green and blue. When red and green lights are both on, those two sensors fire together, which is exactly what happens when you look at something yellow. The screen is tricking your eye.

Part 4 · Sound

Sound is numbers over time

Sound is air wobbling back and forth. A microphone measures how far the air has been pushed, over and over again, and writes down a number each time. Played back quickly enough, the numbers become sound again.

Below is one wobble, made of 32 numbers from −100 to 100. Drag on it to change its shape, then press play. The computer repeats your wobble hundreds of times a second.

Notice that the shape of the wobble changes what kind of sound you hear, and how often it repeats changes how high it sounds. Music on your phone works the same way, with 44,100 numbers for every second of sound.

Why 44,100?Go deeper

The highest sounds people can hear wobble about 20,000 times a second. To capture a wobble you need at least two measurements per wobble (one for the push and one for the pull), so you need a little over 40,000 measurements a second. Music settled on 44,100.

We used numbers from −100 to 100 to keep things simple. Real music usually uses numbers from −32,768 to 32,767, which makes it much smoother.

Part 5 · Words

Words are numbers too

Every letter, digit, space and emoji has an agreed number. When you type, the computer only ever stores those numbers. Type any two words and compare them.

Try:

Look at cat (99, 97, 116) and car (99, 97, 114). The numbers are almost identical, but a cat and a car are nothing alike. Meanwhile kitten means nearly the same as cat, and its numbers look completely different. Even Cat with a capital letter starts with a different number.

These numbers are just labels. They say nothing about what a word means. Much later in the journey (Stop 8) we'll meet a much cleverer way to turn words into numbers, one where the numbers capture meaning.

Every emoji has a number tooGo deeper

A worldwide standard called Unicode gives a number to more than 150,000 characters: every letter in every major writing system, maths symbols, and every emoji. 😂 is number 128,514. That's why a message you send from one phone shows up correctly on a completely different phone in another country.

What you now know

  1. A picture is a grid of pixels. Each pixel is a number for brightness, or three numbers (red, green, blue) for colour.
  2. A sound is a long list of numbers measuring how the air wobbles, tens of thousands every second.
  3. Text is a list of numbers too, but those numbers are just labels. They don't capture meaning.
  4. So any "intelligence" a computer has must come from doing something clever with numbers. What's the simplest possible clever thing? That's the next stop.
